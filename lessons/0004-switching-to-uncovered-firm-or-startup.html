EPFO Optimization Course • Lesson 0004

Switching to a Small Company (<20 Staff) or Starting Your Own Business

When you leave an EPF-covered corporate job to join an early-stage startup with fewer than 20 employees, or launch your own venture, you exit the mandatory EPF system. Here is what happens to your money and your options.

1. What Happens to Your Existing EPF Account?

It Keeps Earning Interest Until Age 58

Under Central Government Notification GSR 1065(E), an EPF account does not stop earning interest when contributions stop. It continues to compound at the official EPFO interest rate (~8.15%–8.25%) until you turn 58 years old.

Tax Catch: While your balance continues to earn interest, court and tax tribunal rulings (e.g. ITAT Bangalore) hold that interest earned after employment ends is taxable at your income tax slab as "Income from Other Sources".

2. Your Two Primary Moves

Move A: Full Settlement (Form 19 + Form 10C)

Since you are no longer in an EPF-covered establishment, after 60 days (2 months), you can apply online for full withdrawal:

  • If prior service was 5+ years: 100% of EPF is tax-free. You can withdraw the entire corpus to fund your startup, invest in index funds, or build emergency liquidity.
  • If prior service was under 5 years: Full withdrawal triggers the 5-year tax trap (employer share and interest taxed as salary + 10% TDS).

Move B: Leave It Parked (Or Voluntary Coverage)

  • Leave it untouched: If you have <5 years service and expect to rejoin an EPF-covered company in the future, leaving the money parked preserves your service record. When you rejoin later, past and future service combine to cross the 5-year tax-free line.
  • Voluntary Coverage (Section 1(4)): If your new small employer or your own startup wants EPF, companies with fewer than 20 employees can voluntarily apply to EPFO to register under Section 1(4).

3. Better Alternatives for Founders & Self-Employed

  • Public Provident Fund (PPF): 15-year lock-in, sovereign guaranteed ~7.1%, 100% tax-free EEE, ₹1.5L/year limit.
  • National Pension System (NPS): Low-cost pension with up to 75% equity exposure and Section 80CCD(1B) tax deduction.
  • Direct Low-Cost Mutual Funds / Index ETFs: Liquid wealth creation for business owners.
